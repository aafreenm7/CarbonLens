Scientific Methodology & Mathematical Formulas

Complete transparency regarding physical energy conversions, lifecycle boundaries, and carbon emission factors.

Critical Accounting Boundary: Double-Counting Prevention

In digital carbon footprint analysis, end-user device consumption can easily be double-counted if operational hardware power is blindly summed with lifecycle services. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA, 2020) methodology, the video streaming factor (\(36\text{ gCO}_2\text{e/hr}\)) encompasses data transmission networks, data centers, and the typical client display device. Therefore, CarbonLens explicitly ensures that laptop or smartphone operational electricity is not duplicated during streaming hours.

1. Electricity Grid Emission Factor

RESEARCH-BASED

Electrical energy consumption is converted into greenhouse gas emissions (\(\text{CO}_2\text{e}\)) using the official weighted-average emission factor for the Indian national electricity grid.

Grid Emission Factor = 0.710 kg CO₂e / kWh   (710 g CO₂e / kWh)
Source: Central Electricity Authority (CEA), Ministry of Power, Government of India (FY2024–25 Baseline).

2. Activity Mathematical Models

A. Laptop Computer Usage

PROJECT MODELLING ASSUMPTION

Operational electricity consumption is based on an active power draw of 50 Watts for a typical business laptop during knowledge work.

Energy (kWh) = [ Usage Hours × 50 W ] / 1000
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Energy (kWh) × 0.710 kg/kWh
Emissions (g CO₂e) = Emissions (kg CO₂e) × 1000
Example: 4 hours = (4 × 50 / 1000) = 0.20 kWh = 0.142 kg CO₂e (142 g CO₂e)

B. Smartphone Usage

PROJECT MODELLING ASSUMPTION

Active operational power consumption is modelled at 2 Watts during screen-on active operation.

Energy (kWh) = [ Usage Hours × 2 W ] / 1000
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Energy (kWh) × 0.710 kg/kWh
Example: 2 hours = (2 × 2 / 1000) = 0.004 kWh = 0.00284 kg CO₂e (2.84 g CO₂e)

C. High-Definition Video Streaming

RESEARCH-BASED (IEA, 2020)

Encompasses end-to-end lifecycle impacts including server encoding, content delivery networks (CDNs), cellular/broadband transmission, and end-device rendering.

Emissions (g CO₂e) = Streaming Hours × 36.0 g CO₂e/hr
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Emissions (g CO₂e) / 1000
Example: 1.5 hours = 1.5 × 36.0 = 54.0 g CO₂e (0.054 kg CO₂e)

D. Email Communication

RESEARCH-BASED (BERNERS-LEE REVISED)

Standard text emails without large attachments are modelled at 0.3 g CO₂e per message, accounting for mail server storage and network routing.

Emissions (g CO₂e) = Number of Emails × 0.3 g CO₂e/msg
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Emissions (g CO₂e) / 1000
Example: 25 emails = 25 × 0.3 = 7.5 g CO₂e (0.0075 kg CO₂e)

E. Generative AI Inference Queries

RESEARCH-BASED (OVIEDO ET AL., JOULE 2026)

Average electrical energy consumed per large language model (LLM) inference prompt is modelled at 0.31 Wh per query, converted using the grid emission factor.

AI Energy (kWh) = [ Query Count × 0.31 Wh ] / 1000
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = AI Energy (kWh) × 0.710 kg/kWh
Emissions (g CO₂e) = Emissions (kg CO₂e) × 1000
Example: 10 queries = (10 × 0.31 / 1000) = 0.0031 kWh = 0.0022 kg CO₂e (2.20 g CO₂e)

3. Projection Modelling (Monthly & Annualized)

When input datasets capture daily work activity, projections are extrapolated mathematically:

Monthly Projection (kg CO₂e) = Daily Total × 30
Yearly Projection (kg CO₂e) = Daily Total × 365
*Note: Projections assume steady-state baseline activity patterns and are explicitly labelled as modelled estimates rather than continuous smart-meter measurements.
