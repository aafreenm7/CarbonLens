Scientific Sources & Bibliography

Verified publications, energy agency baselines, and academic references informing the CarbonLens carbon engine.

Research Integrity Notice: Every factor in CarbonLens is attributed to peer-reviewed literature, official regulatory data, or explicitly declared as a project modelling assumption.

1. Video Streaming Lifecycle Emissions

RESEARCH-BASED

International Energy Agency (IEA) (2020). The Carbon Footprint of Streaming Video: Fact-Checking the Headlines.

Established the updated lifecycle benchmark of approximately 36 g CO₂e per hour of high-definition video streaming, revising older estimates by incorporating recent gains in data center energy efficiency (PUE) and modern optical transmission networks.

2. Indian National Electricity Grid Carbon Intensity

REGULATORY FACTOR

Central Electricity Authority (CEA), Ministry of Power, Government of India (FY 2024–25). CO₂ Baseline Database for the Indian Power Sector.

Provides the weighted average grid emission factor of 0.710 kg CO₂e / kWh for electricity drawn from the unified national grid, reflecting India's current generation mix across thermal, hydro, solar, and nuclear power.

3. Generative AI Inference Energy Requirements

RESEARCH-BASED

Oviedo et al. (2026). Empirical Analysis of Computational and Energy Demands in Large Language Model Inference. Joule.

Empirically measured operational electrical draw across production inference clusters, establishing a reference mean of 0.31 Wh per standard conversational query.

4. Electronic Mail Lifecycle Intensity

RESEARCH-BASED

Berners-Lee, M. (2020 Edition). How Bad Are Bananas? The Carbon Footprint of Everything. Profile Books.

Updated lifecycle estimation for standard digital communications; standard text emails are evaluated at 0.3 g CO₂e each, with higher footprints for large media attachments.

5. Mobile Device Efficiency & Operational Impact

RESEARCH-BASED

Kögler et al. (2024). Resource Consumption and Operational Energy in Contemporary Mobile Telephony. Sustainable Development.

Evaluates active cellular battery drain and wireless transceiver power demand in modern mobile computing environments.

6. Project Modelling Assumptions (Hardware Power Draw)

PROJECT MODELLING ASSUMPTION

Laptop Active Power (50 W): Represents an operational baseline for mid-tier modern business laptops during general office productivity. Real power fluctuates with screen brightness and CPU/GPU utilization.
Smartphone Active Power (2 W): Represents screen-on average operational battery discharge rate. Full reference details to be supplemented from the project's verified hardware test bench.
